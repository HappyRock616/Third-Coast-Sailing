# Changelog

## 0.7.4 (not yet released)

### Fixed
- **Star finder showed no stars on some phones.** The sky was rebuilt for every GPS fix, and on a phone with steady fixes each rebuild was cancelled by the next, so the stars, constellations and names never appeared (the sky stayed at its dark fallback). The finder now takes the position once when it opens and draws the sky once per opening; the rest of the app keeps its live GPS. This also stops the compass smoothing from restarting with every fix.

## 0.7.3

### Added
- **Crash warning.** A red cartoon line drawing of a sailboat snapped in two and sinking into the waves marks the first water shallower than your minimum depth (the water shaded red) that you would hit. On the current heading it looks 30 minutes ahead at your speed (0.5–5 nm), over a narrow ±5° cone in front of the boat using the course averaged over 30 seconds, and only when moving over 1 kn, checked every 20 seconds to save power; the mark is placed straight ahead on the course line. On a plotted route it marks the first shallow spot along the legs. It stops at the first obstruction; no label, no sound.
- **MOB (man overboard).** In the menu just above the GPS box: a soft-red box with a thick red border, the same height as the Boat button. One tap marks the boat's GPS position with a red push pin and clears the chart (wind / gust color, barbs, waves, the route and any weather routing go off; the wind gauge stays as it was). A red line runs from the boat to the pin, labeled true bearing and distance, and the same MOB box sits across the top of the chart: a timer counting up the time in the water (to the second), bearing and distance. An orange drift triangle opens downwind from the pin and grows with time: length = 0.96 % of the forecast wind × time, spread ±0.54 % of the wind × time (US Coast Guard person-in-water leeway, "PIW, unknown state": Allen & Plourde 1999, CG-D-08-99; Allen 2005; see the README for the working and its limits; no current included). Reset by holding the MOB box (menu or chart) for 5 seconds; a darker shade fills across it as you hold. The mark is kept on the phone until reset.
- **Anchor watch.** Menu → *Anchor* (with an on/off switch beside it, and a hold-to-reset arrow while an anchor is saved). Switching on drops the anchor at the boat's GPS position (0 ft: mark it from the bow as the anchor goes down); the Anchor screen can instead place it ahead: point the top of the phone at the anchor, type the distance, *Set ahead* (uses the compass, true bearing). The chart shows the anchor, a bold red circle at the alarm radius (default 100 ft, ±25 ft or typed) and a dashed line of the boat's swing (one GPS fix every 2 minutes). A large yellow box at the top shows distance and true bearing to the anchor, or "GPS fix lost". Outside the circle, or no GPS fix for 10 minutes, the *Third Coast Ships Bell* (made in code) sounds on the alarm channel, which plays even on silent or Do Not Disturb, and the phone vibrates; the box gets a red border and reads **Alarm Silence**. Either volume button (with the app on screen), tapping the box, or *Silence 15 min* on the notification silences it for 15 minutes, then it re-arms. Runs all night with the screen off; closing the app stops the watch. The anchor point and radius are kept on the phone, so after a flat battery and a restart the anchor is still marked: switch the watch back on to resume.
- **Compact menu.** Narrower, with the logo and THIRD COAST / SAILING at the top. Every switch is an icon and one word (Track, Wind, Gusts, Barbs, Waves, Night, Gauge, Offline) with no descriptions. Boat, Celestial (with a small sextant), Charts and About are large orange-bordered buttons with icons at the bottom. Charts (lake downloads and updates, with the yellow not-for-navigation banner) opens its own screen.
- **Chart key** in the menu: buoy fill = wind (0, 8, 14, 20, 25+ kt; gray = no report for 2 h), buoy ring = waves (<3, 3–5, 5+ ft), and the marina, shallow-water, track, route and celestial-fix marks.

### Changed
- **Marina symbol.** Marinas are now an orange rounded tile with an anchor instead of an orange dot, so they never look like buoys.
- **Trip track reset.** Hold the reset arrow (↺) for 1.2 seconds to clear the track (an orange ring fills while you hold; let go early to cancel). A tap no longer clears it.
- **GPS only.** The app no longer uses network (cell / Wi-Fi) location at all.
- **Wave crests drift at every zoom** (they used to stop moving when zoomed out past about one lake). Same battery use: the number of crests depends on screen spacing, not zoom.

### Fixed
- Trip track zigzags and starbursts. Rough network positions, which were mixed in every 5 seconds, are gone (GPS only). The track also leaves out impossible jumps (a fix that would need far more than twice the GPS speed to reach); after three in a row the new position is accepted, so a real move after a GPS gap still shows. Each track point is the moving average of the last 3 GPS fixes. The boat and navigation still use every GPS fix.
- Wind / gust color showed as blocks over land when zoomed out or panned away from the downloaded lakes. The color is now drawn only where the Great Lakes wave model has water, so it stays on the lakes at every zoom.
- Star finder: the 3D boat never appeared. The Android build stores the gzipped boat model unpacked under a shorter name, so the app couldn't find it; it now loads either form.
- Star finder: every star, the constellation lines and the names are now shown day or night (they were hidden in daylight and faded in at twilight), as a learning aid. Navigation stars, planets and the Moon have floating name labels. Below the horizon is now plain solid ground.
- Star finder: the orange arrow now works at every level and points to the body the finder was opened for (or one you tap). Off screen it shows the way to turn, worked out from bearing and altitude so it no longer flips when the body is behind you, with a label giving its name, bearing and altitude, or "below the horizon now".

## 0.7.2

### Added
- **Trip track.** A *Trip track* switch in the GPS section of the menu records a thin dashed line of where the boat has been, labeled with distance and elapsed time (e.g. "12.4 nm · 1:23", updated every minute). Switching it off stops recording and the clock but keeps the line; switching it back on continues it. The line is continuous: any gap (switched off, phone asleep) is spanned by a straight line and its distance counts, while time spent switched off doesn't. Once there's a track, a reset arrow (↺) appears beside the switch to clear it. Nothing is saved between app sessions. Points are recorded every 15 m (50 m when nearly stopped, so drifting at the dock adds nothing). While Trip track is on and the app is in the background or the screen is off, the position is still sampled every 5 minutes; Android shows a "Trip track recording" notification during that time. GPS isn't used in the background at any other time, and swiping the app away stops it.
- **Celestial navigation trainer.** Menu → *Celestial navigation trainer*. A yellow "Training · not for navigation" banner sits at the top. Pick a skill level (Beginner, Intermediate, Advanced); it applies to all three tabs:
  - **Plan:** today's dawn, sunrise, noon, sunset and dusk times. Beginner and Intermediate also get what to shoot and when, with preset altitude and bearing, plus *Find in sky* and *Add to workbook* buttons. Beginner adds tips and *How to take this sight ›*, which opens the sextant guide. Advanced gets the times only.
  - **Workbook:** one card per sight. The next shot's entry is always showing, headed "Shot 3" (and so on): tap **MARK TIME · SHOT 3** the instant the body touches the horizon and it writes the time, to the second, into the time box; then type the sextant reading in the angle box and tap *Enter ✓*. Either box can be typed by hand (a time from your watch, or a correction). Take 3–5 shots: they're averaged, and any off the trend are struck through with one sentence saying why (for example "5.2′ higher than the line through your other shots, likely a misread drum or the body held above the horizon, so it isn't used."). The working is laid out line by line the way a navigator writes it, each line with its equation:
    - almanac values at the hour, plus the minutes-and-seconds increment and the small v/d corrections;
    - index correction, dip, refraction, parallax and semi-diameter, giving the observed altitude;
    - assumed position, LHA, calculated altitude and azimuth;
    - intercept (miles toward or away).
    
    Levels:
    - **Beginner:** filled in, with a *From the almanac* drop-down showing the table rows the numbers came from.
    - **Intermediate:** filled in, with those rows always shown.
    - **Advanced:** you type every line from the Almanac tab. A wrong line is struck through and outlined red, with no explanation and no correction; the line of position and fix are plotted from your own numbers.
    
    At Beginner and Intermediate, *Open the page ›* and the › beside each almanac line open the almanac at the page and row that number came from.
    
    Height of eye defaults to 9 ft (standing on deck). Beginner picks from a drop-down (sitting in the cockpit 6 ft, standing on deck 9 ft, standing on the cabin top 11 ft); the other levels type any height. There's also a box for index error. With no GPS, type a DR position. Sights are kept between app sessions.
  - **Almanac:** a book, laid out like the printed almanac (paper-colored pages, serif type, hours in groups of six, yellow increments pages), one UT day per page: Sun & Moon, Aries & planets, Stars, Increments, Dip & refraction. ◀ ▶ turn the day; pinch to change the type size. *Search* jumps to a body ("rasal"), a date ("Oct 18"), a UT time ("23:46"), an increments page ("46m") or "dip 9", and also lists your own sights of a body.
  - **Sextant guide:** one step per screen with a big picture and large text, for the body you're shooting (Sun, Moon, star or planet). Swipe or use Back / Next; the dots and *Steps ▾* jump straight to any step. The step you were on is remembered for 15 minutes (until the app is closed), so you can switch to the workbook and come back to it. *Read the sextant* has a second picture, a close-up of the micrometer drum and vernier with the reading written underneath (22 . 4′). Times are marked in the workbook, like on paper. Opened from *How to take this sight* on the Plan tab or *Guide* on a sight.
  - **On the chart:** lines of position and the fix are drawn in purple, labeled with the body and time. Tap a fix to open the workbook.
  - **How good is the fix?** At the bottom of the workbook: the error compared with GPS (distance and true bearing from the GPS position; against the typed DR when there was no GPS), and a circle the true position should be inside about 95% of the time, also drawn round each fix on the main chart. The circle comes from how each sight was rated and how many shots it had, and from how widely the lines cross; if the lines disagree more than their ratings suggest, it's enlarged and the workbook says so. Each sight (Intermediate and Advanced) has a *Conditions* drop-down, defaulting to Moderate: **Easy** (calm, steady deck, sharp horizon), **Moderate** (some motion, fair horizon), **Hard** (rolling hard, rain or spray, hazy or dim horizon, or the body under 15°). Harder sights count for less in the fix. At Beginner level every sight is Moderate. The sextant guide has a new last step, *Rate the conditions*. All bearings in the trainer are true (°T).
  - **Plotting aids.** Each sight's reduction ends with a small plot, drawn north-up like a plotting sheet: the assumed position (AP), a dashed line toward the body at its true bearing (Zn) with an arrowhead and ★, the intercept in orange, the line of position at right angles, and the boat at its GPS / DR position on its course. Beside it: "Line of position · Zn 231.9°T · 7.1 nm away from Rasalhague" and a color key. Tap it for a large labeled plot (with a scale) and the four plotting steps worked for that sight. The fix card has a plot of the fix: every line, an arrow from the fix toward each body with its bearing, the 95% circle, the GPS (or DR) position as a square, and the boat on its course. The boat is only for orientation: its heading never changes a line. The sextant guide gets three plotting steps with pictures: *Where you looked*, *Plot the line* and *Cross the lines for a fix*.
  - **Fix log for the trip:** *Save fix ✓* on the fix card keeps the fix and its workbook and starts a fresh workbook for the next one. Saved fixes stay on the chart, joined by a dashed purple line with each leg labeled true bearing and distance ("084°T · 8.4 nm"). The *Workbook ▾* drop-down at the top lists *Current* and each saved fix by its time, and opens saved workbooks read-only. Everything is kept on the phone through app restarts for the length of a trip (not exported). The reset arrow (↺) appears once anything is entered and, after a confirmation, clears the current sights, every saved fix and the fix log.
- **3D star finder** (*★ Sky* in the trainer, *★ Find* on a sight, or *Find in sky* on a suggestion). Hold the phone up and the sky turns with it, using the phone's compass with magnetic variation applied. It's for a rough look only, not for measuring.
  - **Sky:** shaded for the real time, date and place, without the camera. It runs from blue day through an orange glow on the horizon under the Sun at twilight (with the pink band opposite the Sun just after sunset), to dark night. Stars come out as it darkens. Constellation figures are drawn, with names, and so are the navigation stars, planets and Moon.
  - **Boat:** a 3D model with the round logo and "Third Coast" on both sides and its navigation lights on. It lies on its last course inside a compass ring fixed to north, seen from behind in the direction you face. A vertical line runs from the boat up to a box showing where the phone points (bearing and altitude).
  - **Using it:**
    - Beginner: an orange arrow points to the suggested star.
    - Any level: tap a star, planet or the Moon to select it, then *Use* or *Add sight*.
    - Pinch to zoom; on devices with no compass, drag to look around.
    - ✕ or Back returns to the workbook.
- **Celestial navigation trainer: calculations.** Built-in almanac for the Sun, Moon, Venus, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn and the 58 navigational stars (checked against JPL's DE421 ephemeris: within 0.3 arc-minutes); full sight reduction from sextant reading to line of position (index error, dip, dip short of a shoreline, refraction with temperature and pressure, parallax, semi-diameter); fixes from two or more sights, including running fixes for a moving boat; daily sight planning following Bowditch (twilight star windows, the best three stars about 120° apart with preset altitude and bearing, shooting order, morning / noon / afternoon Sun, Sun–Moon daytime fixes); star finder data with constellation figures and names; printed-almanac style tables for working by hand; three skill levels (beginner step-by-step, intermediate suggestions, advanced tables only); and eight sextant step pictures.
- **About & credits** (bottom of the menu): version, the not-for-navigation statement, the name ("Third Coast" is a common name for the Great Lakes; no affiliation with any business or organization), free and open source (AGPL-3.0, link to the code), and every data source and library with its terms, including the OpenStreetMap attribution. The README has the same name note.
- **First-launch notice.** Before anything else, a one-time "Before you start" notice says the app is for reference only, not for navigation, and must be accepted ("I understand").
- **Workbook: air temperature (°F) and pressure (inHg)** for the refraction correction (Intermediate and Advanced; defaults 50 °F, 29.83 inHg).

### Changed
- **Adding and moving route points.** "+" now drops the next point straight away, a quarter of the screen height ahead (so the spacing follows the zoom): the first point goes along the boat's course (straight up the screen when stopped or without GPS), later points continue the last leg. To move a point, hold it for about half a second without moving (the phone buzzes), then drag; quick taps, swipes, pans and pinches never move a point. Routes are limited to 6 points (the "+" fades at the limit).
- **Minimum depth per boat.** Boat setup has a new *Minimum depth* dropdown (6, 12, 18, 24 or 30 ft; default 24 ft). The weather router and the chart shading both use the active boat's setting.
- **Shallow water is shaded red, with one red line at the limit.** Water shallower than the minimum depth gets a light red fill instead of red outlines around every shallow area. This removes the confusing extra lines at chart seams, dredged channels and band edges. A single red line marks the charted contour at the minimum depth (from zoom 8 inward).
- **Disclaimer banner.** A yellow "Not for navigation · Reference use only" banner sits at the top of the Charts section of the menu.
- **Selection ring.** A blue ring marks the marina or buoy whose info card is open.
- **Lower battery use with waves on.** The wave-crest animation updates about 10 times a second instead of every screen frame (60–120 times a second). It looks the same and uses a fraction of the power.
- **Smaller app.** The APK now carries the map engine only for ARM phones and tablets (64- and 32-bit), dropping the two x86 emulator copies: about 27 MB smaller (74 MB → about 47 MB). The app no longer installs on x86 emulators or Intel Chromebooks.

### Fixed
- The wind gauge's "target" speed label could be cut off at the edge; it now sits on the inner side of the dot and stays inside the gauge.
- The weather box read "Updated updating…" during an update; it now reads "Updating…" (and "Never updated" before the first one).
- The heading box showed "---°" with no course yet; it now shows a dash.
- The router treated water starting exactly at the limit (for example 24–30 ft areas with a 24 ft limit) as too shallow, because of feet-to-meters rounding. It now matches the chart shading.
